# Islands rebuilt as tropical atolls (v2)

`IslandShape` gains an atoll layer on top of the coastline it already generated: a sandy outer ring whose width varies with bearing (`ringWidth`), an off-centre inner island (`coreRadius`, with a `coreShift` cosine term), and a lagoon annulus between them opened across two or three angular windows (`lagoonWindows` / `lagoonBand`) with land bridges in between. The bay lobe moved out into that annulus to become an anchorage, the river became a tidal pass that stops at the inner island, and the landmark is anchored to the inner island's centre rather than to a fraction of the coast radius. The boarding island gets a special case: an open harbour notched through the sand ring (`ringNotch`) with a minimum rim (`lagoonMinRim`) sized from the raft berth grid. The bay fit moved after the river and lake, so `MAX_WATER_FRACTION` now actually fires. The spec was rewritten to match; all 301 assertions pass.

**Watch for:** 6 of 13 islands are no longer one landmass — the sand ring is severed from the inner island and ships as 2-3 separate islands; `raid_cove` has **no** continuous land corridor at any bearing, so its village and volcano are unreachable on foot from every beach (**confirmed**). `_fitLagoon`'s documented invariant ("a continuous band of land survives at EVERY bearing … keeps the island one walkable landmass") is not what the code measures, and no assertion covers it (**confirmed**). The boarding island's four berths now sit 476-702 studs out, slot 1 entirely in open ocean 168 studs beyond its own coast (**confirmed**), and its harbour rim is pinned to a perfect circle on 360 of 360 bearings — with the irregular-rim assertion skipped exactly for that island (**confirmed**). `self.coreMax` is read in `_fitLagoon` and never assigned anywhere (**confirmed**). Several rewritten assertions can no longer fail: `ringNotch ~= nil` is true by construction, the shelf flatness check measures ground the shelf itself flattens, and the river pass check accepts open sea found sideways (**confirmed**).

## High-level view

The atoll silhouette itself lands: the coast is measurably non-circular, ring width and core rim both vary with bearing, the core is off centre, nothing is a dome, and the ASCII maps read as sand arms wrapping a lagoon around a jungle island with a cone on it. Determinism is exact — two builds of all 13 islands produce zero differing cells and identical `coreBase`, `buildableRadius`, `villageX` and `lagoon.baseR`.

What the ring layer broke is walkability. The lagoon annulus is open across 566-703 of 720 bearings on every island, and the only thing holding the arms onto the inner island is the handful of bearings where a window closes or the core overruns the ring. Those bearings are not required to survive the bay lobe or the river pass cutting across them, and on six islands they do not: `raid_cove` ships as three separate islands (48% / 29% / 21% of its land), `mistwood` as three (51% / 28% / 21%) joined only by a single 54-stud corridor, plus `quiet_sand`, `tide_flat`, `riverlands` and `drift_shack` with 8-35% of their land stranded offshore. `_fitLagoon`'s geometric clamp only measures the bay lobe against the outer coast, so the invariant its own comment claims is neither enforced nor asserted.

The boarding island's open-harbour approach fixes the "rafts inside terrain" failure from the last pass — all four berths float. It also moves the anchorage outside the island: the harbour centre sits 589 studs out where the coast is 534, the nearest land is 252 studs away, and slot 1's whole deck-plus-pad footprint is open ocean 127 studs past the outer edge of the submerged approach shelf. Nothing shelters those rafts, and `collideRaft`'s `safeHarbor` exemption — written when crews started inside the island's own water — now leaves the largest island in the game, with its 260-stud volcano, passable to rafts.

The `coreMax` / area-cap interaction is fragile rather than wrong today. The rim floor overrides everything `_fitLagoon` does to the bay (`baseR` is shrunk 237 → 75, then discarded by `max(rim, lagoonMinRim)`), so the water cap's only remaining lever is growing the inner island — which it does, 0.459 → 0.516 — after `harbourDistance` was already computed from the pre-growth core rim. The core stops 16 studs short of the basin; at the 0.56 ceiling it would reach 17 studs inside it and put berth 4 back on land.

The settlement fitter is the strongest part of the change: all 13 islands find real ground on the inner island, `buildableRadius` lands between 66 and 135 studs, and `VillageBuilder`'s `buildable / 1.3` cap keeps street rings and waterfront rows inside the clearing. What is not derived from the shape is which way the town faces — `harborFacingAngle` is a hand-authored constant defaulting to 0, so the "waterfront" house row points 8-175 degrees away from the nearest water, including on the arrival hub whose comment says it looks across its own harbour.

The spec was rewritten in the same commit as the geometry, and four of the rewrites removed the only assertion that would have caught what shipped. The bay-enclosure check became an arms-walling check that probes the middle of the sand arm; the landmark off-centre rule (`>= half * 0.10`) became a from-core rule that `mistwood` passes at `0.045 * half` from the island centre; the sea-to-sea river check became an outer-stations check accepting `ocean` and `shelf` inside a ±181-to-244-stud lateral sweep; the "berths in the sheltered bay" check became "the anchorage is not land". River width went the wrong way since the last pass: the clamp tightened from 93.5 to 62 studs on every 1,100-stud island, against a 64-stud raft with a 52-stud collision pad, while `Config` still labels `RiverWidth = 440` the raft contract.

### The sand ring is not attached to the inner island

The atoll's land is two structures: the ring (outward of `coastRadius - ringWidth`) and the core (inward of `coreRadius`). They meet only where `lagoonBand` returns nil — when the window weight drops to zero on a land bridge, when the core rim overruns the ring's inner edge, or when the band would be thinner than `half * 0.03`. Over 720 bearings:

```
                 open lagoon  closed window  core overruns ring  too thin
raid_cove            703            0                 0             17
mistwood             702            0                 0             18
riverlands           663            0                25             32
quiet_sand           623            0                77             20
buccaneer_flat       566            0               120             34
```

Those non-lagoon bearings are the only candidate bridges, and nothing protects them: the bay lobe and the river pass may cut anywhere that is not on the core, so a bridge bearing can be land at the arm, land at the core, and water in between. Walking every bearing from just inside the core rim to just inside the beach and requiring all-land:

```
raid_cove        NO continuous land corridor at ANY bearing
mistwood         1 corridor,  54 studs wide
riverlands       2 corridors, 103 and 33 studs
kings_hold       3 corridors, 179, 46 and 18 studs
buccaneer_flat   2 corridors, 313 and 155 studs
```

A 4-connected flood fill over land cells at 8-stud resolution confirms what that implies (8-connected gives the same answer, so this is not a grid artefact):

```
                 pieces >= 200 cells
arrival_hub      13,815 (r=2..733)
raid_cove         3,559 (r=3..348, centre) | 2,163 (r=317..529) | 1,549 (r=299..508)
mistwood          3,857 (r=0..487, centre) | 2,160 (r=268..530) | 1,577 (r=311..519)
quiet_sand        4,791 (r=2..540, centre) | 2,057 (r=271..530) |   534 (r=300..506)
tide_flat         5,817 (r=3..536, centre) | 1,373 (r=301..501) |   821 (r=281..527)
riverlands        5,826 (r=0..530, centre) | 2,353 (r=281..512)
drift_shack       6,777 (r=1..546, centre) |   579 (r=311..493)
```

`arrival_hub`, `smuggler_hut`, `crow_cay`, `buccaneer_flat`, `kings_hold`, `wreck_spit` and `lantern_port` are single masses. On the six above, an arm reached by raft is a dead end: the beach is a separate island from the village, the volcano, the settlement loot and the villagers. `raid_cove` is the flagship town isle and the worst case — its core carries the village and the cone and touches no arm at all.

`_fitLagoon`'s header claims this is handled:

> 1. A continuous band of land (plus its beach) survives between the bay rim and the outer coast at EVERY bearing. That keeps the island one walkable landmass instead of a set of disconnected arms

The loop below it (`:456-478`) marches outward from the bay centre, stops when the point passes `coastRadius(angle) - margin`, and converts that limit back into a bay `baseR`. It measures the bay against the outer coast and nothing else. The atoll band that severs the arms did not exist when the comment was written.

### The boarding island's anchorage left the island

`spawnBasinRadius(4, 160, 2, 64, 52)` is 237 studs, and the harbour is placed at `coreRadius(lagoonAngle) + basin + beachWidth * 1.5` = 589 studs from the island centre, where the coast on that bearing is 534 and the submerged shelf ends at 575:

```
slot 1  r=702  coast=534  shelf ends 575   9/9 footprint samples ocean
slot 2  r=604  coast=577  shelf ends 618   ocean 4, shelf 2, water 3
slot 3  r=595  coast=565  shelf ends 606   ocean 4, shelf 2, water 3
slot 4  r=476  coast=535  shelf ends 576   ocean 1, water 8
nearest land to the anchorage centre: 252 studs
```

Only slot 4 is in harbour water; slots 2 and 3 straddle the submerged approach shelf, and slot 1 is in open sea 168 studs beyond its own island's coast. Two things follow. Crews no longer start in anything that reads as a harbour. And `collideRaft` (`IslandService.luau:2790`) still skips the arrival island entirely, on the reasoning that "crews are placed inside the arrival island's sheltered water, so its shoreline guard is omitted; its real terrain banks still fence the raft in visually" — the premise is gone, so the exemption buys nothing and lets a raft drive through 1,500 studs of island and a 260-stud volcano.

The rim floor also makes the harbour a circle. `lagoonRadius` returns `max(harmonicRim, lagoonMinRim)`, and after `_fitLagoon` shrinks `lagoon.baseR` from 237 to its floor of 75, the harmonic term maxes out at `75 * 1.55 = 116` — below the 237 floor on all 360 sampled bearings. The bay's four-harmonic profile is computed and then discarded. The assertion that would have caught this is the one the same commit disabled for `ringNotch` islands.

### `coreMax`, the area cap, and fit order

Moving `_fitLagoon` after the river and lake means `MAX_WATER_FRACTION` fires for the first time — `arrival_hub` 0.374 → 0.307, `raid_cove` 0.341 → 0.307, `drift_shack` 0.327 → 0.310, `smuggler_hut` 0.250 → 0.240. That closes issue 8 from the last pass. The rest of the catalog sits at 0.23-0.31 against the 0.32 cap, so most islands are one water feature away from triggering it.

The loop's first lever is `self.coreBase = min(coreBase * 1.06, self.coreMax or 0.56)`. `self.coreMax` is assigned nowhere in the repository, so the ceiling is a constant reached through a nil field.

Growing the core is not free. Forcing `coreBase` to 0.56 and re-measuring:

```
                 open lagoon bearings      ring swallowed
riverlands           663 -> 391             168/720
lantern_port         637 -> 415             224/720
mistwood             702 -> 431             142/720
buccaneer_flat       566 -> 460             220/720
```

At the ceiling the inner island reaches the arms across a fifth to a third of the compass and the lagoon annulus disappears there — reducing the water fraction by deleting the lagoon. Today only `arrival_hub` (0.516) and `raid_cove` (0.480) grow at all, so this is headroom rather than a live defect, but the loop has no term that notices.

On the boarding island the same growth is a berth hazard. `harbourDistance` is computed at construction from `coreRadius(lagoonAngle)`, then `_fitLagoon` grows `coreBase` by 12%:

```
basin inner edge  = 589 - 237 = 352
core rim on that bearing      = 336   (was ~307 before the growth)
margin                        =  16 studs
at coreBase = 0.560           = 369   -> 17 studs inside the basin
```

Berth 4 sits at r=476 with its inward pad corner near 400, so a further ~30 studs of core growth puts land under it. Nothing in the fit knows the basin exists.

### Determinism

Two independent builds of all 13 islands agree exactly: zero differing cells over the full sample grid, identical `coreBase`, `lagoon.baseR`, `buildableRadius` and `villageX`. Neither fitting loop draws randomness, so they cannot perturb the stream. Draw order does couple to the option flags — the `o.lagoon ~= false` block consumes about twenty draws, the river block two, the lake block three — so toggling `River` or `Lagoon` on a catalog entry re-rolls that island's landmark facets, ridge yaw, lake and shelf jitter. Stable per entry, and worth knowing before someone flips a flag and wonders why the island moved.

### What the rewritten spec no longer catches

Four assertions were replaced in the commit that introduced the geometry, and in each case the replacement is satisfied by construction or by the thing being measured.

The bay-enclosure check (`enclosed >= 40` land bearings around the bay rim) became "sandy arms wall the lagoon in on most bearings", which probes `outer + ringWidth * 0.5` — by definition the middle of the sand arm, land unless the bay, the pass or the notch cuts exactly there. It cannot detect a bay that is a bite out of the coast, and it says nothing about the arms being attached.

The landmark rule went from `lmOffset >= half * 0.10` to `fromCore >= half * 0.03`. Measured `lmOffset / half`: `mistwood` 0.045, `smuggler_hut` 0.089, `wreck_spit` 0.089, up to 0.162 on the hub. Three islands would have failed the old rule, which is why it was replaced. `mistwood` is the case that matters: a cone 25 studs from the island centre, a small core shift, and a ring broken by a single 54-stud corridor — a bullseye by the module's own definition.

The river check went from "channel runs sea-to-sea" (`blocked == 0`, requiring `water`) to outer stations only, accepting `water`, `shelf` or `ocean`, scanned across a sweep of `meanderAmp * 1.8 + width` — 181 studs on the 1,100-stud isles, 244 on the hub. A station 300 studs out can find open sea 244 studs sideways and pass. All seven river islands currently satisfy it with genuine channel water, so nothing is masked today.

The boarding checks became `anchorKind ~= "land"` and `ringNotch ~= nil`. The second is set unconditionally at `IslandShape.luau:320` whenever `spawnBasinRadius` is passed, so it asserts that the code above it ran.

The shelf flatness check (`:505`) samples the village footprint through `sample`, which applies the shelf flattening itself — measured tallest ground is 1.4-1.6 studs on all 13 islands against a 12-stud threshold. And `beaches are a shoreline band` allows sand up to 75% of land; the atoll's arms put actual values at 0.53-0.65, leaving little room before it fires on a legitimate silhouette.

### Settlement shelf gaps

The fitter finds usable ground on every island (`buildableRadius` 66-135, every footprint sample on land, no island falling back to the `(0, 0)` / `half * 0.07` minimum), and no catalog island uses the `wild` theme — so the narrower `shelfSearchRadius` path and the unvalidated `(0, 0)` fallback are exercised only by endless islands and are untested.

The town's facing is not derived from the shape. `harborFacingAngle` defaults to 0 and is set once, to `math.pi`, on the arrival hub. Comparing the waterfront row's direction against the bearing to the nearest water from the village centre:

```
raid_cove       off by   8 deg
crow_cay        off by  53 deg
arrival_hub     off by  64 deg   (comment: "looks north across its own harbour")
riverlands      off by  90 deg
drift_shack     off by 116 deg
lantern_port    off by 148 deg
quiet_sand      off by 175 deg
```

All rows land on flat ground inside the clearing, so nothing is embedded in terrain. They face inland, with the bay the shelf fitter deliberately scored toward behind them.
